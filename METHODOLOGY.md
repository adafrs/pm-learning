# How these articles were researched

Every article in this repository went through the same source-quality loop. The goal is advice you can trust and act on, not advice that only sounds plausible.

## The loop (applied to every key claim in every article)

1. **Draft the claims.** List the specific, practical pieces of advice the article intends to make.
2. **Find at least two independent, credible sources per claim.** Sources are preferred in this order:
   - Peer-reviewed research (journal noted; citation counts where visible)
   - Books by recognized authorities with large readerships (bestseller status, ratings volume)
   - Established publications: Harvard Business Review, MIT Sloan Management Review, Stanford Social Innovation Review, Nonprofit Quarterly, Chronicle of Philanthropy
   - Widely read practitioner sources: SVPG (Marty Cagan), Lenny's Newsletter, Mind the Product, Product Talk (Teresa Torres), Nielsen Norman Group
   - Community signals (for example repeated recommendations on r/ProductManagement or r/nonprofit) are used only as **supporting** evidence of reach, never as the sole support for a claim.
3. **Look for counter-evidence.** Each topic was searched for critiques, failed replications and "this doesn't work in small organizations" arguments. Claims that did not survive were dropped or qualified. You'll find this in each article's *Where this advice breaks down* and *Evidence check* sections.
4. **Open every cited URL.** Each source was fetched to confirm it exists and says what the article claims. Nothing was cited from memory alone; no quotes, statistics or URLs were invented.
5. **Paraphrase.** Direct quotes are short and attributed.
6. **Translate to practice.** Everything is rewritten for one reader: the only product manager at a small nonprofit, working through an Executive Director, a board, program staff, volunteers, beneficiaries and funders.

## How to read the "evidence strength" label

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | Multiple peer-reviewed studies or meta-analyses agree, and practitioner sources are consistent with them. |
| **Moderate** | Strong practitioner consensus plus some research support, or research that is solid but from a different context (for example large corporations rather than small nonprofits). |
| **Mixed** | Credible sources disagree, findings have replication problems, or the evidence is mostly expert opinion. Use with judgment. |

## Each article contains

- A one-paragraph summary
- Why the topic matters for a solo nonprofit PM
- Numbered practical advice with inline citations
- A two-week action checklist
- Limits and critiques
- An evidence-check table (claim, support, counter-evidence, verdict)
- A source list with a "why trusted" note for each source
- An **AI study prompt** you can paste into Claude or another AI assistant to be quizzed on the article

## Keeping it current

Each article has a `last_verified` date in its front matter. Links rot and research moves on; re-check sources older than about a year before relying on them for a big decision.
